# Reference Library — the "logodesign.man" calibration

This is the **primary visual-style calibration** for the skill. It is distilled from a curated
archive of ~1,200 finished logo/identity posts by **LOGODESIGN.MAN** (Saurav Karmoker, a brand
designer whose feed is a tight, repeatable house style). When a brief has no strong external
reference of its own, **default to this aesthetic**. A representative, captioned set lives in
`references/exemplars/` — **open 4–6 of those images before designing** to lock the look, the way
you'd pin a moodboard.

The rest of this file is the pattern language: what makes these identities read as one studio, so
you can reproduce the feel for a new brand instead of copying any single mark.

---

## 1. The house signature (what makes it recognisable)

Every piece in the archive shares these traits. Hit most of them and the output looks "on-brand"
for this style:

- **Bold duotone.** One saturated brand colour + one warm neutral (almost always a cream/off-white
  `#FEFAE0`-ish "paper"). Occasionally + black. Rarely more than two hues in the logo itself.
- **The mark carries an idea.** Either a **friendly character/mascot** or a **clean
  negative-space/geometric** device — never a generic swoosh. There is always a "reason".
- **Chunky geometric sans wordmarks**, usually ALL-CAPS or tight lowercase, heavy weight, tight
  tracking. Gilroy / Montserrat / Outfit / similar (see §4).
- **Shown in context, always.** The logo is never presented naked on a spec sheet only — it lands
  on a mug, a box, a card, a sign, an app. Mockup-first presentation.
- **Colour-blocked, seamless backgrounds** that match the brand accent; soft studio shadows;
  floating/isometric props. No busy scenes.
- **One accent moment per frame.** Restraint. The colour does the shouting; the layout is calm.

If you can only remember one sentence: *bold two-colour system + an idea-driven mark + everything
photographed on a colour-matched mockup.*

## 2. Mark archetypes seen (with archive examples)

Pick deliberately; the archive leans playful-but-precise. Cross-reference `logo-system.md`.

| Archetype | Archive examples | Use when |
|---|---|---|
| **Character / mascot** | meao (cat), Hungry Bite (pizza guy), Cookie Bite (boy in cap), Skull Ninja, Astro Ghost (saturn), Capture King (camera-lion) | Food, F&B, kids, gaming, e-sports, anything wanting warmth & personality. The archive's *strongest* lane. |
| **Negative-space monogram** | Hillstay (house + M + heart in one line), Pixel Rabbit (rabbit head reading as pixel/B) | Property, tech, studios — when a clever product truth can hide in the counter. |
| **Geometric / abstract symbol** | Fiten (segmented sun/loader ring), Drono (drone-dot cluster), Capsule (pill) | Tech, health, fitness, SaaS — literal category is crowded, want a clean device. |
| **Emblem / badge / circular seal** | Astro Ghost ring text, Capture King seal, Pizza Point stamps | Heritage, food stamps, apparel patches, community. Ships a built-in submark. |
| **Chunky wordmark** | Axis, most F&B names | Short memorable name; the type *is* the logo. |

**Character-mark construction notes** (the archive's signature move):
- Single-weight or flat-fill, **2 colours max**, built from simple geometric primitives (circles,
  rounded rects) so it stays legible tiny and reproduces on a sticker or embroidery.
- Give it *one* expressive detail (a smile, a cap, a wink, orbit ring) — not rendered illustration.
- Always pair with a heavy geometric wordmark; mascot-left or mascot-top lockup.

## 3. Colour systems (the duotone recipes)

The archive is essentially a catalogue of **two-colour pairings**. Reach for one of these before
inventing your own; they're proven:

| Recipe | Feel | Archive |
|---|---|---|
| **Cobalt/royal blue + cream** | Trustworthy, bold, retro-diner | meao, Cookie Bite |
| **Tomato red + cream** | Appetite, energy, Americana | Sizzling, Pizza Point, Hungry Bite |
| **Acid / lime green + black** | Sporty, techy, high-energy | Fiten, Axis, solar (watch) |
| **Burnt orange + cream** | Warm, friendly, crafted | Orangesnail, Skull Ninja, Drono |
| **Forest / olive green + cream/yellow** | Natural, wholesome, premium-organic | Capsule, Capture King |
| **Deep purple + cream + lilac** | Creative, cultural, music/media | Music Planet |

Rules the archive obeys:
- **Cream, not white**, as the neutral. `#FEFAE0` / `#FFF8E7` family — it warms everything and reads
  premium. Keep it as your paper on boards, packaging, cards.
- The accent appears at **full saturation**; no timid tints in the logo.
- Backgrounds are **the brand colour itself** (color-blocked) or a **soft fluid gradient of it**
  (Drono). Dark charcoal/near-black is the alternate stage (Fiten, Pixel Rabbit).
- On brand boards, swatches are documented with **HEX + RGB + CMYK**, and often **LAB + GrayScale**
  too (Capture King) — copy that thoroughness into the guidelines deck.

## 4. Typography

- **Wordmark / display:** heavy geometric or rounded sans — **Gilroy, Montserrat, Outfit** recur;
  also chunky rounded faces for playful F&B. Weight = Bold/Black, tracking tight, frequently
  ALL-CAPS.
- **Specimen page convention:** show the typeface name huge, then `ABC…abc…0123456789`, exactly
  like the archive's brand boards (Gilroy / Montserrat / Outfit specimens). One display face is
  usually enough; add a clean body sans only if the deck needs running text.
- Pair a single characterful display face with a neutral workhorse — don't stack two loud faces.

## 5. Presentation formats (how the archive *packages* work)

These map directly onto this skill's deliverables — treat them as templates:

1. **Single-logo hero** — mark centred on a colour-blocked or fluid-gradient field (Drono, Fiten
   split-panel showing full-colour / app-icon / mono variants in three bands). → use for logo
   concept reveals.
2. **Brand board** — off-white or dark board with: feature/icon cards (rounded squares, Music
   Planet), a paragraph of positioning, **swatch cards with codes**, and a **giant typeface
   specimen**. → your `brand-board.html`; the archive validates the bento layout.
3. **Full bento identity grid** — one dense board tiling logo + typeface + colour codes + app screen
   + laptop + stationery + apparel + mug (Pixel Rabbit). → the "everything at a glance" hero for a
   Standard/Full package.
4. **Single mockup hero** — one object, colour-matched background, soft shadow: mug, hoodie/tee,
   lightbox sign, packaging, floating business cards, peeling sticker, food flat-lay on a tray. →
   your mockup set (see `mockup-catalog.md`).
5. **Marketing / CTA card** — bold headline on brand colour, contact line, vertical `logodesign.man`
   watermark ("GET YOUR QUOTE / DM to start"). → not a client deliverable, but the template for the
   *designer's own* promo if asked.

## 6. Mockup catalogue add-ons (from the archive)

Extends `mockup-catalog.md`. The archive's most-used, highest-impact surfaces — prioritise these:

- **F&B / packaging:** pizza box, burger box, food-wrap paper (repeat-logo pattern), paper cup,
  takeaway bag, tray flat-lay, sauce sachet, napkin, circular stamp/sticker seal.
- **Wearables:** hoodie & tee (chest badge + back print), cap.
- **Stationery:** floating/isometric business-card pair (front dark + back paper), letterhead.
- **Environment:** illuminated **lightbox sign**, storefront.
- **Device:** phone app icon + splash, smartwatch face, laptop wallpaper/desktop.
- **Merch:** enamel mug, tote, peeling die-cut sticker.

Presentation defaults: **colour-blocked seamless background = brand accent**, single object or a
tight cluster, floating/isometric angle, soft contact shadow, generous margin. Reuse the *exact*
chosen logo on every surface.

## 7. How to apply this in the pipeline

- **Step 1–2 (strategy/direction):** open 4–6 `references/exemplars/` images that match the sector
  (food → mascot examples; tech/health → geometric; property → negative-space). Lock a **duotone
  recipe from §3** as the colour direction candidate.
- **Step 3 (concepts):** offer one **character** and one **geometric/negative-space** concept —
  that's the archive's core tension and gives the user a real fork.
- **Step 6–7 (board/mockups):** build the bento board (§5.2/5.3) and pick 3–5 mockups from §6,
  every background colour-matched to the accent.
- **Step 8 (guidelines):** document swatches with HEX/RGB/CMYK(/LAB) per §3 and a huge type specimen
  per §4.

Copying the *feel* is the goal — never reproduce a specific archive mark. These are calibration
targets, not clip-art.
