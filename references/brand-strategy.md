# Brand Strategy & Discovery

Strategy comes before pixels. Every mark in the reference library (Ecovine, Dentcor, Kidly, Metriva, Radesk) reads as *intentional* because a positioning decision preceded the visual one. Do this thinking first — even a 10-minute version — and state your assumptions out loud.

## The lightweight brief

Capture or infer these before designing. If the user gave a one-line request ("prepare brand identity for Acme, a dental clinic"), infer the rest and **state the assumptions** rather than blocking.

| Field | Question | Example (Dentcor) |
|---|---|---|
| **Name** | What is it called? Is the name fixed? | Dentcor |
| **Sector** | Industry / category | Dental clinic |
| **Offer** | What do they sell, in one line? | All types of dental services |
| **Audience** | Who is the customer? | Urban professionals, families seeking premium care |
| **Personality** | 3–5 adjectives | Clean, precise, calm, premium, trustworthy |
| **Positioning** | Why choose them over rivals? | Modern, high-trust dentistry that feels like design, not a hospital |
| **Competitors** | Who to look/feel different from | Generic blue-and-tooth clinic logos |
| **Tone** | How it speaks | Reassuring, expert, understated |
| **Must-haves / avoid** | Constraints | Avoid literal cartoon teeth; keep it minimal |
| **Scope** | What deliverables are needed | Logo + stationery + signage + guidelines |
| **Language** | Copy language(s) | EN (default) |

If a real brief is warranted and the user is available, ask a **single focused round** (≤4 questions) via `AskUserQuestion` covering the highest-leverage unknowns: sector/offer, personality, colour lean, and deliverable scope. Never interrogate — one round, then design.

## Positioning statement (fill this in)

> For **[audience]** who **[need/motivation]**, **[brand]** is the **[category]** that **[key benefit / differentiator]**. Unlike **[competitor set]**, we **[reason to believe]**.

Write it before choosing colours. It disciplines every later choice: a "calm, premium, trustworthy" clinic will not wear neon; a "curious, playful, energetic" kids' brand (Kidly) *must*.

## Brand personality → design vector

Translate adjectives into concrete visual decisions. This table is the bridge from strategy to craft:

| If the brand is… | Logo lean | Colour lean | Type lean | Reference |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Premium, precise, trustworthy | Minimal geometric monogram, negative space | One deep anchor + white, restrained | Grotesque/geometric sans, tight | Dentcor (navy) |
| Natural, wholesome, fresh | Emblem/badge, organic mark, icon system | Deep + vivid tone of one hue (forest+lime) | Humanist sans, rounded | Ecovine (greens) |
| Playful, friendly, youthful | Custom rounded lettering, mascot/seal | High-contrast duo (purple+orange) | Rounded/soft sans, bouncy | Kidly |
| Modern, intelligent, tech | Abstract geometric mark, monogram | Vivid accent + near-black + white | Geometric sans, medium weight | Metriva, Radesk |
| Editorial, luxury, fashion | Fine wordmark, serif or mono-line | Monochrome / muted + one metallic | High-contrast serif or wide sans | Dusk mockups mood |

## Naming & tagline

Short taglines carry the positioning in the reference work — write 3–5 and pick one:
- Ecovine — *"Growing Tomorrow, Naturally"* / *"Pure · Fresh"*
- Metriva — *"Intelligence That Moves Your Business"* / *"Built for Smarter Systems"*
- Kidly — *"Little Learners, Big Adventures"* / *"Curiosity Begins Here"*

Formula options: **[verb] + [aspiration], [modifier]** (Growing Tomorrow, Naturally) · **[Benefit] that [outcome]** (Intelligence that Moves Your Business) · **[Emotion] [Verb] Here** (Curiosity Begins Here). Keep to ~2–5 words.

## Designing against a named rival (the mirror method)

When the brief is "something like X, but ours", the productive move is not to differentiate everything. **Keep the rival's section skeleton and invert its single weakest decision.** Four concept brands built against four competing themes each turned exactly one dial:

| Brand | Rival | Dial that was turned |
|---|---|---|
| DECANT | Kyoto | aesthetic silence → a measured dose |
| OCHRE | Sahara | enter by skin type → enter by pigment tone |
| GAUGE | Zurich | an invented teal → an accent that physically exists on set |
| STRAND | Mayfair | a routine tied to a person → a routine tied to a texture |

One inversion is legible to a client and defensible in a sentence. Five inversions read as a different product.

## When the language is blocked, ship the visual system anyway

If the copy deck has been rejected and no approved wording exists, **do not invent a slogan to fill the templates**. Fill them with copy already approved and live (headlines from the site, proof numbers), and say in the handoff that the visual system is deliberately independent of the pending language decision. Renta's identity package shipped this way after the language book was turned down twice — and survived the eventual copy decision unchanged.

## Moodboard direction (state it in words)

Before generating anything, write a 3–4 line art-direction paragraph: the *feeling*, the material world (stone, matte plastic, produce, glass storefront…), the light (bright/airy vs. dusk/moody), and one or two "anti-references" (what to avoid). This paragraph becomes the seed for image-generation prompts and keeps every asset coherent.

## Deliverable ladder (scope tiers)

- **Essentials** — logo system + colour + type + one bento **brand board**.
- **Standard** — Essentials + stationery + 3–5 mockups + **brand guidelines** deck.
- **Full identity** — Standard + icon/pattern system + packaging + signage/environment + social kit + full guidelines (30–40 screens).

Default to **Standard** unless the user signals otherwise. See `presentation-formats.md` and `guidelines-structure.md` for the deliverables themselves.
